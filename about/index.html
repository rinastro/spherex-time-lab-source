The story behind the Time Lab

SPHEREx Time Lab is our answer to the “Planet X and SPHEREx” challenge of the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge: a public web tool that shows how the SPHEREx sky changes over time.

The challenge

Too much sky for one person

Since 2025 SPHEREx has mapped the whole sky every six months in 102 near-infrared bands. Comets, asteroids, nearby stars and brown dwarfs reveal themselves by shifting between images. The brief: let anyone enter a position and see its images play like a short film.

Our approach

Simple to start, honest when measuring

One sky atlas and one play button are enough to begin. When you want numbers, the lab measures the original FITS pixels and records where every value came from.

What we built against the brief

  • Enter RA and Dec, or an object name, and play every matching SPHEREx exposure as an animation.
  • Includes presets for the five targets named in the brief: Barnard’s Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855−0714, RW Aurigae and R Draconis, each one click away.
  • Easy to update: exposures are queried live from IRSA, and the release (DR1 or QR3) is a single setting.
  • The 102 bands: the sky atlas shows any channel of the DR1 all-sky cube, searches can target one detector, and the science lab builds spectra and light curves from the exposures.
  • Subtracting images taken at different times: a difference view of the current frame minus a reference frame, computed from the displayed PNGs for visual inspection.
  • Set the mosaic zoom in the URL or choose the exposure cutout size in the search form.
  • Overlays from another mission: Gaia DR3 stars moved with their proper motions to the date of each frame.
  • Query by URL and embed the lab in other websites.
  • Metadata on screen: observation times, wavelengths, quality-flag maps and a downloadable record for each series.
  • Built on public tools: Astropy for FITS and WCS, Aladin Lite from CDS for the sky atlas.

Data and credits

This publication makes use of data products from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), which is a joint project of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the California Institute of Technology, and is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration.

SPHEREx Data Release 1 spectral images: doi:10.26131/IRSA678. SPHEREx DR1 all-sky spectral cube: doi:10.26131/IRSA679. Data are served by the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive.

This work has made use of data from the European Space Agency (ESA) mission Gaia (cosmos.esa.int/gaia), processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC). Funding for the DPAC has been provided by national institutions, in particular the institutions participating in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement.

The sky atlas uses Aladin Lite, and object names are resolved with Sesame, both developed at CDS, Strasbourg Astronomical Observatory, France.
