How it works

From coordinates to a moving dot

Choose

Pick a patch of sky

Click the sky atlas, type RA and Dec, look an object up by name, or start from one of the five challenge test targets.

Find

Find SPHEREx exposures

The lab asks NASA/IPAC’s Infrared Science Archive which Data Release 1 images cover that spot, then cuts the same field out of each one.

Compare

Blink, subtract, measure

Play the frames on one north-up grid, subtract two dates so that fixed stars cancel, and measure brightness in the science laboratory.

Why motion matters

Pluto was found by blinking two photographs.

Comparing images of the same field taken at different times is one of the oldest discovery methods in astronomy. SPHEREx maps the whole sky every six months in 102 near-infrared colours, which is far more than anyone can inspect alone.

The Time Lab makes that inspection quick enough for anyone to help, and keeps every step traceable to the archive.

In the lab

Tools you will find

Sky atlas

SPHEREx DR1 all-sky map

Browse the released DR1 sky cube in any of its spectral channels and see which exposures cover the map centre.

Blink

Time series player

Exposures sorted by UTC time and shown on a common sky grid with one brightness scale, so differences are not caused by the display.

Difference

Current minus reference

Brightness is converted back to MJy/sr before subtracting. A moving object leaves a dark and a bright spot.

Gaia DR3

Catalogue overlay

Gaia stars are moved with their proper motions to the date of every frame, so fast stars land where they really are.

Science lab

Photometry and spectra

Point-source fits on the original FITS pixels give fluxes with errors, spectra across wavelengths and light curves.

Share

Links, embeds, records

Coordinates live in the URL, the lab can be embedded in other sites, and every series can be saved with its provenance.
